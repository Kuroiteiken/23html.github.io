Echoes Beneath development history

Changelog

Player-facing releases, gameplay improvements, content additions, and fixes.

This fork is now called Echoes Beneath. It began as a fork of the upstream Proto23 game and keeps its save format, its repository name, and its stored preferences, so existing saves and language settings continue to work.

Early alpha — systems and save compatibility may change.

Release

v478

v477 → v478 2026/08/17
  • The level 35 reward from the dojo instructor was broken: accepting it gave you the shield and then left you stuck on the same screen with no way back, handing over another shield every time you pressed Accept. It was trying to give a pill that does not exist. Level 50 had the same fault
  • The Hoplite, Knight and Dread shields now defend. All three were listed as rare and had no stats at all, so reaching level 35 earned you a rare shield that protected you exactly as well as an empty hand
  • Carrying a shield now helps rather than hurts. A shield's resistances were being taken off your protection instead of added to it, and taken off your armour's share as well as its own — so a better shield and more Shield training both increased the damage you took. Nothing changes if you fight without one
  • SPD now increases, one point every ten levels. It is the stat that makes attacks miss you, and nothing in the game had ever raised it: it sat on 1 from the first level to the last
  • LUCK now increases, one point every five levels. It raises your critical hit chance and every drop roll in the game — creature drops, rank drops, gathering and pickpocketing
  • An existing character is paid what these two would have given them, once, the first time the save is opened. A level 40 character does not start over at SPD 1 behind a fresh one
  • The dojo's training dummies are set to the student in front of them. They were pinned at levels 10 to 13 — below the dojo's own first trial at 20 — so the moment you were good enough to be sent at a golem, the room you trained in had nothing left to teach you
  • The deep catacombs keep pace with you. Their two hunting grounds now follow your level up to the mid forties, which is where the story says the dark ki has been pooling. Nothing there gets easier and no first visit gets harder: the levels you meet on the way down are exactly the ones that were written, and what waits at the very bottom is the same fight it always was
  • You can sell what you are carrying. The general store buys anything now, priced from the same supply lists the shops sell from, so what he pays and what he charges cannot drift apart. Before this the only things in the game with a buyer were firewood, straw baskets and cure grass — everything else a hunter came home with could only be thrown away
  • He pays well under what he charges, and no better than that however good you get at haggling, so there is nothing to be made buying from him and selling straight back. Quest items and whatever you are wearing are never on the list
  • Every price in the stall of odds and ends was NaN. The kid was the one vendor who never set a markup and there was no default, so nothing there had a price, the check for whether you could afford it passed anyway, and buying something turned your entire purse into NaN
  • The sell list scrolls inside the shop panel instead of running off the bottom of the window. With a full inventory it used to spill past the panel and over the game's own tabs
  • The game no longer shrinks itself to reserve room for the save bar. The bar lays out correctly on its own, so the reservation cost scale for nothing
  • The wolf pack leader at the hollow can be fought. It could not be hurt at all — every blow landed for nothing while it took your health down — and it was not alone: the ghast, the zombie knight, the unchanging skeleton, the mummy and the disaster corpse were all armoured past the point where any weapon in the game does anything
  • They are all still the hardest things in the game, but through health and strength rather than by being immune. None of them got weaker against elements or against the dark; what changed is the plain physical armour that had been set four to six times too high, and the attacks, which had been given the same numbers again by mistake
  • Every creature in every area is now checked against the toughest thing the game originally shipped, so this cannot happen again
  • How hard a creature hits is checked the same way, not only how much it shrugs off. The same numbers that made those six untouchable were also making them hit far harder than anything the game originally asked you to face
  • Every shield in the game defends now. Eleven of the seventeen were listed with a rarity and nothing else, so a Tower Shield protected you exactly as well as an empty hand, and none of them had a description
  • The Casserole Shield exists again. It had been declared under the Kite Shield's own name, so it overwrote it at startup and neither the pot lid nor the kite shield were both in the game at once
  • Turkish: the Kite Shield was named after a kite on a string. It is a shield shape
  • Shield mastery grows while you carry a shield, not only when something hits you. It was the skill that makes a shield worth carrying and the only way to train it was to be struck
  • The eight weapon and shield mastery titles above the second tier can be earned. Sword, Axe, Polearm, Hammer and Shield each hand out their first two ranks and then had nothing above them. Each of the new ones also makes that mastery grow faster from then on
Release

v477

v476 → v477 2026/08/17
  • Six more titles now actually do something. Only four of the game's hundred and eight ever did, and two of those were the running discounts — so Runner and Sprinter, earned by far more running than Walker and Jogger, gave nothing at all. Omnivore now judges spoiled food, the Campfire Cook can cook without the fixture a recipe calls for, the Animal Friend teaches the cat faster, and the Brawler hits harder bare-handed
  • When Yamato tells you to rest, one night is now enough. The board wanted the date to change twice, so doing exactly as you were told and sleeping once brought you back to nothing
  • Leaving the game running in another tab now keeps up to eight hours of progress instead of one, and catches up as fast as your machine can rather than a minute at a time. If you died while away, the rest of that time is discarded rather than fought from a corpse
  • The dojo instructor has rewards past level 30 again — at 35, 40, 45 and 50. Everything he hands over already existed in the game with no way at all to obtain it, and none of it raises a combat number
  • "Potions consumed" never appeared in your statistics no matter how many you drank: the panel was reading a counter nothing writes to. Four more things the game has always quietly counted are shown now, including kills that finished from poison or bleeding — which turns out to have had a title waiting for it with no way to earn it
  • New side story at the market stalls. The nervous man has stood there with nothing behind him since before this fork, and the game has been quietly remembering whether you once pressed him or let him be. If you let him be, he has something to tell you about Dein
  • One kind of message in the log could come out too dark to read. The marketplace crier picked its colour completely at random, so roughly one shout in several was invisible
  • The journal has a fourth section: what you have worked out about the world. That tab has been sitting there showing "????????????" with nothing behind it since long before this fork
  • It holds the clues you have found, the questions the story has opened, and the answers you have earned — and it leaves an unanswered question standing rather than hiding it, so you can see the shape of what you still do not know. Nothing appears in it until you have actually seen it
  • The catacombs go all the way down. The western corridor and the rooms beyond it are open, and The End Of Journey — the room the map has always finished on — is where the chapter lands
  • New quest, The End of Journey. Yamato wants to know how far the passage runs and what is at the bottom of the part a man can walk with a candle
  • Ghasts, zombie knights, zombie mages, mummies, unchanging skeletons and the disaster corpse can now be fought. All six were defined with a rank and nothing else — no health, no stats, no drops
  • What is standing at the end explains why any of this is happening now, and the answer was already written into the game: a disaster corpse only appears where dark ki has been pooling, and an unchanging skeleton harms nobody until death ki has worked on it long enough
  • The last thing you find down there is not a monster. Someone marked the way before you and went down, and Yamato knows exactly whose mark it is
  • The Ruin Medallion, Essence of Night and Soul Stone can now be obtained. None of them had any source anywhere in the game
  • Running and scouting now keep going while the tab is in the background. They advanced on a timer of their own, which the browser throttles to about once a minute, so they quietly stopped making progress even though the rest of the world was catching up properly
  • Coming back after a long absence now reports the whole gap, instead of announcing the first minute of it and then saying nothing for however long you were actually away
  • Turkish: seven pairs of different things shared a single name. Poison and Venom were both "Zehir" despite being separate statuses with separate resistances, Coal and Charcoal were both "Kömür", and Runner and Jogger were both "Koşucu" — which matters, since those two carry the running discounts
  • The catacombs go deeper. Past the Web Corridor the eastern ring holds the ones that still remember how they used to fight, alongside a ghoul that is too fast to corner — neither can be handled on the other's terms
  • Searching works down there now. The exploration track under the catacombs was written and then left with nothing to find; what it turns up is what the village has been losing, including the shopkeeper's lantern. It costs candle time, like everything else in the dark
  • Eleven catacomb room names were badly mistranslated into Turkish. The rooms had never been reachable, so nobody had ever read them: the Web Corridor was named after the internet rather than a spider, the Forgotten Post after mail rather than a sentry, and the Stone Plate was a dinner plate
  • The catacombs are open. Twenty-six finished rooms existed under the village with nothing in the game leading to them; the way in is now the wall at the back of your own cellar, and the way out brings you back to it instead of to the village centre
  • New quest, Beneath the Village. It is an investigation rather than a hunt: three signs in any order — what the old shopkeeper has been saying for a month, what the marketplace says instead, and what your own cellar wall looks like once you actually hold a light to it
  • Cave bats, stirges and zombies can now be fought. They were defined with a rank and nothing else — no health, no stats, no drops — so they could not have appeared anywhere even if somewhere had wanted them
  • The torch can now be found. It was the only thing in the game besides candles that lets you see in the dark, and nothing anywhere sold it or dropped it
  • Yamato now tells you what darkness actually does to a hunter, and to buy candles before you go down. Nothing in the game had ever explained it
  • A long inventory no longer pushes its own controls out of the way. The filter row stays at the top and the sort buttons and coin counts stay at the bottom; only the item list scrolls between them
  • A weapon's kill count moved onto its class row, held to the right. It used to be drawn on top of the rarity row, interleaving the two into one unreadable line
  • The area readout no longer covers the battle controls above it. A long area name could wrap it onto a second line and bury them
  • Coming back to a newer build now opens with a short note on what changed since the version you last opened. It is remembered separately from your save, so it shows once even if you never press Save, and a first-time player never sees it
  • The save bar no longer covers the game's bottom row of buttons, and it is styled to match the rest of the game instead of a light grey strip with its text dimmed along with the background
  • Shop buying price and reputation now sit on the bottom edge of the shop panel. The panel asked for a share of a container that has no height of its own, so nothing below it resolved: the stock list grew with the shop's stock instead of scrolling, and the strip holding the two readouts collapsed to no height at all
  • The story continues past the wolf hunt. Yamato said to expect to be contacted later, and now he means it: a day after you collect the reward, his own commission is waiting on the lodge board
  • New quest, The Pack Leader. The wolves you killed were starving and sick — every wolf in the game is a Weakened Wolf — and something drove them north out of the deep south. The hollow past the foliage is a third southern scene, and what dens there is the one wolf that was never weakened
  • The hollow leads with what is written on the ground rather than a fight, because Yamato asks you to look before you kill
  • The Wolf Mask can now be obtained. It had no drop, recipe, or vendor anywhere in the game
  • Fixed the wolf's scratch doing nothing. It named an attack that does not exist, so it silently became an ordinary bite with no bleed chance and no damage bonus
  • The running titles now discount the running cost itself, so a 5% title costs 0.095 per second and a 15% title costs 0.085. The discount used to be applied to a stored rate instead
  • Fixed the energy drain climbing a little with every run. The cost was charged when you started and refunded when you stopped, so earning a running title in between refunded less than it had charged and the difference stayed on the rate for good. Running no longer writes to the stored rate at all
  • Existing saves are corrected on load, so a save carrying that residue stops draining while you stand still
  • The energy readout now shows three decimals, so a discounted cost of 0.085 is no longer displayed as 0.08
  • Fixed Run disappearing from the Actions panel after the panel had been opened once
  • Fixed titles applying their permanent bonus a second time when an older save was loaded
  • The changelog header now carries the game's emblem

v476

v475 → v476 2026/08/17
  • Standing still no longer costs energy. Energy is spent by what you do: running, taking hits, and bad weather. Running still costs 0.1 per second, reduced by the running titles
  • Existing saves are corrected on load, so a save that carried the old idle drain stops draining while you stand still
  • Fixed the energy drain rate creeping up permanently every time you started and stopped running. Opening the Actions panel left the previous rows in place, so one click could start running twice while stopping it refunded once
  • Returning to a backgrounded tab now says how much time passed. Energy drains every tick, so catching up several minutes at once looked like it was draining for no reason
  • Mastery levels are now saved. Every level bought was silently lost on reload, because mastery progress was never written to the save at all
  • Observation and Reflexes now have descriptions and actual effects; both were spendable but did nothing. A hidden fourth branch, Second Wind, opens once Physical Training and Athletics are fully mastered
  • Ten titles that could never be earned are now awarded: the three job titles from completed work, four collecting titles, and three toughness titles from damage survived. All of them already had counters running behind them
  • A damaged save is now checked before anything is restored. Previously a shifted or truncated save could load as the wrong data; it is now backed up untouched and reported instead
  • Every creature now has a rank, so the bestiary's rank column reads properly instead of showing '???' for almost half the roster
  • Hovering a bestiary entry now shows what the encyclopedia says about that creature; the book previously listed only a name, a rank, and a kill count
  • Lowered the message log limit ceiling from 120 to 50; a longer log cost more than it gave back

  • A save that cannot be read is kept as a backup and reported instead of being replaced silently by a new game
  • Imported saves are now stored properly instead of being lost on the next reload
  • Damaged saves no longer restore missing statistics as blank values
  • Fixed the "pause next battle" label showing the wrong state after loading a save
