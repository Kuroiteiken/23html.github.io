Echoes Beneath development history

Changelog

Player-facing releases, gameplay improvements, content additions, and fixes.

This fork is now called Echoes Beneath. It began as a fork of the upstream Proto23 game and keeps its save format, its repository name, and its stored preferences, so existing saves and language settings continue to work.

  • v478.1 — everything below, from the pack leader onward
  • The game now numbers its point releases. Small updates between the big ones get their own number, and opening the game tells you about the ones you missed rather than only about the last release
  • The smith sharpens weapons, +1 through +9. Each step adds a little to what the blade is worth, so a good weapon gains more from the work than a poor one, and a +9 blade hits about half again as hard as it did
  • It gets dearer and less certain as it climbs: the first step is near certain and costs almost nothing, the ninth is close to a coin toss and costs many times the weapon. A failed attempt takes the fee and changes nothing — your weapon is never destroyed and never set back
  • The level shows on the name wherever the name appears, and the colour climbs with it, so a glance at your pack says which blade is the worked one
  • The four statues go in the house instead of on your body. A straw effigy, a wooden idol, a bone doll and the soul puppet were all worn in the accessory slot, and now they are furniture — standing one in the corner is worth exactly what wearing it was worth, and taking it back down hands you the statue again
  • They also stack in your pack now instead of taking a slot each, which is what makes crafting the soul puppet out of the other three straightforward
  • The nightmare happens. Sleeping in your own bed can now pull you into one, written and commented out since long before this fork because the thing in it has a hundred million health and never attacks — so the fight it started could be neither won nor lost
  • It is not a fight. You are awake and cannot move, and it is standing at the foot of the bed waiting. One choice, always there and never failing: try to wake. Waking costs you the rest of the night and nothing else
  • Staying is worth something. Holding still trains Patience, and breathing what put the thing there trains Dark Absorption — and it thins out on its own after ten hours whether you wake or not. It only finds you once the story has explained what came up under the village
  • The same line repeated no longer fills the log. It collapses into one row with a count on the right — x2, x3, x40 — so crafting a stack or landing the same blow twenty times does not push everything worth reading out of the log, which only keeps the last few dozen lines
  • Crafting has x5, x10 and All. Clicking the recipe still makes one, which is what the row is for; these are for when you are holding forty of something and want all of it turned into something else. "All" works out how many you can afford in one go rather than counting up from one
  • Things can be set on fire. A blow that lands with fire behind it — a torch, the Scorpion Sceptre, or any ability that carries the element — can catch, and then keeps burning for a few seconds after. A scratch rarely catches and a heavy hit usually does
  • Every creature in the game has carried a resistance to burning since long before this fork, and nothing had ever read one, because there was no burning to resist. Now they do
  • Every title you can earn from the second rank up now carries something, fitted to what the title is for: a mastery that grows faster, a point or two of a stat, a better chance at a critical hit. Twenty-one of them had been names and nothing else
  • Including the ones you earn for going hungry or nearly dying, which give you what those states would actually teach — you learn to run on less, and surviving what should have killed you leaves you harder to kill
  • The village centre and the marketplace can be searched. Seven places were attached to a search table that had been written and then commented out, so none of them could be searched at all. What a market loses is small change and the things nobody would buy, and that is what turns up
  • Repairing something at the smith no longer leaves you at the bench with no way back out
  • And an item's description no longer sticks to the screen after the row it belonged to is gone — it used to hang there until you happened to hover over something else
  • Four more things to put in the house, and the general store sells them. All four already existed with names and descriptions in both languages and no way on earth to obtain one: a good blanket that says it helps you sleep and never did, a talisman for the window, a faded painting, and a potter's failed piece that its own description says was meant to be displayed
  • There is a smith at the market, and he repairs things. Durability is on every piece of equipment you own and wears down as you use it, and until now nothing in the game brought it back — a weapon that ran out was simply spent, and swinging it did about as much as swinging your fist. He charges by how worn the thing is, so keeping a blade in order is cheap and letting one run to nothing is not
  • Sleeping a full night beside a burning fire leaves you Rested for a while after you get up: a little more strength, a little more speed, and mastery coming ten percent faster. Six hours by the fire is a night; less than that is a nap, and a nap already paid you in healing
  • Going to bed stops describing a crouch on the floor once you own a real bed. The game hands out straw bedding at the start and a proper bed can be bought later, but the words never noticed which one was in the corner
  • Resting beside a lit fire heals half again as fast and returns a little energy. The fireplace had no effect on resting at all, which is most of why there was never a reason to keep the woodpile stocked
  • Opening the game shows a loading screen instead of a blank background. There had been one all along, but it was built by the game's own code near the end of a file that is itself the slowest thing the page loads — so it could not appear until everything it was meant to cover had already finished, and it was taken away about fifty milliseconds later
  • It tells you where it has got to: reading the language files, loading the game, restoring your save, and — when there is one — applying an update to it. A save migration used to report itself to the browser console alone; it now says so on the way in and again in your message log
  • It is written in your language from the very first frame, before any of the game exists to translate anything. Both languages are put into the page when the site is built, straight out of the language files, and the one you are not reading is removed
  • An item's description now says what it actually gives you. It listed STR, AGL, INT and SPD and stopped, so every resistance in the game was invisible: the Wolf Mask has always been twenty points of fire protection and read like decoration, and a shield's class values, the whole reason one shield differs from another, were never shown at all. Nothing changed about the numbers — they are simply legible now
  • The check that pulls a new build through a stale cache was looking in the wrong place and quietly doing nothing. It asked for the version file beside the loader instead of beside the page, got a 404, and treated that as “nothing to do” — so the protection added a build ago was never actually on. It says so out loud now if the file is ever missing again
  • The fireplace can be built up. Adding fuel replaced what was already burning with whatever you just threw on, so a stick worth fifteen minutes dropped a coal fire worth five hours down to fifteen minutes, and the blazing and roaring states it describes could never be reached by anything but one enormous log. It accumulates now, up to a day’s burning
  • “You feel the warmth of the fireplace” is finally shown. The line was written, translated into both languages, and then overwritten by a second handler fifteen lines below it, so it had never once appeared
  • The quest list is in an order. What you are working on comes first, then what you have finished, each in the order you took it on. It had been sorted by a rule that compared one quest’s progress against another’s position and gave no answer at all for most pairs, so a finished quest could sit above an active one and the arrangement changed for no reason
  • A fight left running in another tab keeps fighting properly. Only the faster of the two was landing blows: the slower one’s attack was on a half-second timer, and a background tab slows a timer to about once a minute, so sixty rounds went by with sixty of its blows queued up behind them. At low levels the slower one is you, which made an unwatched fight deadlier than the same fight watched
  • And an unattended fight no longer loses most of its time. Catching up was given twelve milliseconds per wake-up, which is plenty when one second is owed and nowhere near enough for the sixty a hidden tab owes — a combat second is expensive. The shortfall carried forward, grew on every wake, and past the eight-hour ceiling was simply discarded. Catching up now gets the time it actually needs
  • Running, searching and reading were checked too, one at a time. All three were already keeping up; nothing about them changed
  • The message log controls fit their strip. The two toggles and the clear button ran together into one unreadable line, because a label that was too long for its box simply carried on over its neighbour. They are shorter now, and clipped so a label can never be somebody else’s problem again whatever a translation does to its length
  • The six animal masks give a little of the element they symbolize on the attacking side, not only in defence. Each already resisted its own element strongly — the Wolf Mask has always been solid fire protection — but nothing carried the other half, so wearing one never made you better at the thing it stands for
  • A new build reaches you without a hard refresh. Everything the game loads already carried a version stamp, but index.html itself did not, so a cached copy kept asking for the previous build’s files and no amount of reloading helped. The page now asks the server which build is current and fetches itself again, once, if it is behind
  • Turkish: Polearm Mastery was named after a pole rather than a weapon, which made it hard to recognise in your own skill list. It is Kargi Ustaligi now, shortened for the list the way the English name is
  • The level 35 reward from the dojo instructor was broken: accepting it gave you the shield and then left you stuck on the same screen with no way back, handing over another shield every time you pressed Accept. It was trying to give a pill that does not exist. Level 50 had the same fault
  • The Hoplite, Knight and Dread shields now defend. All three were listed as rare and had no stats at all, so reaching level 35 earned you a rare shield that protected you exactly as well as an empty hand
  • Carrying a shield now helps rather than hurts. A shield's resistances were being taken off your protection instead of added to it, and taken off your armour's share as well as its own — so a better shield and more Shield training both increased the damage you took. Nothing changes if you fight without one
  • SPD now increases, one point every ten levels. It is the stat that makes attacks miss you, and nothing in the game had ever raised it: it sat on 1 from the first level to the last
  • LUCK now increases, one point every five levels. It raises your critical hit chance and every drop roll in the game — creature drops, rank drops, gathering and pickpocketing
  • An existing character is paid what these two would have given them, once, the first time the save is opened. A level 40 character does not start over at SPD 1 behind a fresh one
  • The dojo's training dummies are set to the student in front of them. They were pinned at levels 10 to 13 — below the dojo's own first trial at 20 — so the moment you were good enough to be sent at a golem, the room you trained in had nothing left to teach you
  • The deep catacombs keep pace with you. Their two hunting grounds now follow your level up to the mid forties, which is where the story says the dark ki has been pooling. Nothing there gets easier and no first visit gets harder: the levels you meet on the way down are exactly the ones that were written, and what waits at the very bottom is the same fight it always was
  • You can sell what you are carrying. The general store buys anything now, priced from the same supply lists the shops sell from, so what he pays and what he charges cannot drift apart. Before this the only things in the game with a buyer were firewood, straw baskets and cure grass — everything else a hunter came home with could only be thrown away
  • He pays well under what he charges, and no better than that however good you get at haggling, so there is nothing to be made buying from him and selling straight back. Quest items and whatever you are wearing are never on the list
  • Every price in the stall of odds and ends was NaN. The kid was the one vendor who never set a markup and there was no default, so nothing there had a price, the check for whether you could afford it passed anyway, and buying something turned your entire purse into NaN
  • The sell list scrolls inside the shop panel instead of running off the bottom of the window. With a full inventory it used to spill past the panel and over the game's own tabs
  • The game no longer shrinks itself to reserve room for the save bar. The bar lays out correctly on its own, so the reservation cost scale for nothing
  • The wolf pack leader at the hollow can be fought. It could not be hurt at all — every blow landed for nothing while it took your health down — and it was not alone: the ghast, the zombie knight, the unchanging skeleton, the mummy and the disaster corpse were all armoured past the point where any weapon in the game does anything
  • They are all still the hardest things in the game, but through health and strength rather than by being immune. None of them got weaker against elements or against the dark; what changed is the plain physical armour that had been set four to six times too high, and the attacks, which had been given the same numbers again by mistake
  • Every creature in every area is now checked against the toughest thing the game originally shipped, so this cannot happen again
  • How hard a creature hits is checked the same way, not only how much it shrugs off. The same numbers that made those six untouchable were also making them hit far harder than anything the game originally asked you to face
  • A blow that lands is never worth nothing now. Protection is taken off your attack as a flat amount and the result was floored at zero, so anything whose armour happened to exceed your whole output stopped taking damage entirely — not slowly, never — and the log just read "total 0" with nothing to do but walk away. A hit that connects now always counts for something
  • The wolf pack leader gives more ground to a spear and a hammer than it used to. Note that a weapon you have not trained is far weaker than one you have: the mastery for the weapon in your hand multiplies your strength, so switching classes costs you most of your damage until you train the new one
  • Selling something no longer leaves you stuck in the list with no way back out, and keys are no longer offered for sale — a key is worth nothing until it meets its lock
  • Mastery in the weapon you are holding now tells against your target's armour as well as your own strength. Each level takes a point off the class resistance standing between you and the thing you hit — training a weapon taught you where the gaps in a guard are. It cannot take that resistance below zero
  • Before this, armour was subtracted whole whatever you knew, so against a well-armoured target a weapon you had trained for years and one you picked up an hour ago came to nearly the same nothing
  • Every shield in the game defends now. Eleven of the seventeen were listed with a rarity and nothing else, so a Tower Shield protected you exactly as well as an empty hand, and none of them had a description
  • The Casserole Shield exists again. It had been declared under the Kite Shield's own name, so it overwrote it at startup and neither the pot lid nor the kite shield were both in the game at once
  • Turkish: the Kite Shield was named after a kite on a string. It is a shield shape
  • Shield mastery grows while you carry a shield, not only when something hits you. It was the skill that makes a shield worth carrying and the only way to train it was to be struck
  • The eight weapon and shield mastery titles above the second tier can be earned. Sword, Axe, Polearm, Hammer and Shield each hand out their first two ranks and then had nothing above them. Each of the new ones also makes that mastery grow faster from then on
